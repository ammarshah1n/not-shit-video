# Generated human footage: optional lane

The recovered emotional brief called for one consistent character before and after a change: overwhelmed at work, then genuinely present in an ordinary life moment. It explicitly excluded presenter avatars and synthetic talking-head ads. This lane is separate from deterministic product capture.

## Keep identity and causality

1. Establish a character reference sheet: face, hair, wardrobe, age range, posture and lighting. Use a fictional adult or an explicitly authorised likeness.
2. Make approved still keyframes before generating motion. Use the same identity/reference inputs for each shot. First/last-frame controls and image-to-video help preserve continuity.
3. Write one physical action per shot, one camera instruction, consistent lens/lighting, and the emotion visible in behaviour. Do not pile a full scene change into a four-second generation.
4. Inspect hands, eyes, mouth, reflections, objects, background text and continuity. Regenerate the bad shot; do not hide it under rapid cuts.
5. Make the product visibly cause the relief. Otherwise the film is two unrelated stock-looking montages.
6. Use generated imagery for atmosphere or narrative, never as a fabricated customer testimonial or a false recording of the real product.

## Provider selection

Recheck current official model availability, regions, pricing, duration/resolution limits, commercial terms and retention before buying anything. The old research listed many vendors; those prices/models are not a current purchasing guide. Compare only the few candidates that support the required identity and shot control. No paid calls or credit purchases without approval.

Do not redistribute source reference photos, stock footage, model outputs, music or voice samples without checking the actual applicable rights. Do not clone an identifiable voice or face without the person's permission. This pack contains no paid generated media and no provider credentials.

## Finishing

Conform fps and colour before composition. Avoid gratuitous optical-flow interpolation around hands and text. Use brand type as real vector/raster overlays in the compositor, not text hallucinated by a video model. Derive vertical edits intentionally; do not simply crop the speaker or proof off-screen.
